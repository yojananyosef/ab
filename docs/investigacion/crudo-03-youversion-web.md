#### ⭐ D.2 — LICENSING: what you may and may not do with their content. This is the critical section.

**Two separate regimes. Understand which one applies to you.**

**(a) Reading/using their content as an end user** — `help.youversion.com/…/copyright` (Copyright FAQ), verbatim key points:

> "We recognize the desire to distribute the content to as many people as possible. However, because we have specific license agreements with our content providers, **we can't give permission for complete republishing and distribution.** We invite you to share **a link** to YouVersion's products, websites, and social media channels with your audience instead."

> "**YouVersion can't give you permission to share content outside of our platforms, including in print.** The content of each Bible Plan and related scripture is owned by its copyright holders."

> **Allowed, explicitly:** sharing bible.com deep links to specific verses on social media; sharing **Verse of the Day Images** "freely with your networks" (with the caveat: "they can't be used, reproduced, or sold for any for-profit product or service"); the **Bible App for Kids** videos for church/school classrooms (curriculum provided).

> **Refused, explicitly:** republishing / screencasting / recording audio of Bible Plans, videos or other content onto your own site, blog, YouTube, podcast or social channels; **printing** a Bible Plan for a small group (use "Plans with Friends" instead); overdubbing into another language.

Also: *"The copyright holder can be found at the bottom of each chapter."* Attribution rules live per-publisher, not in YouVersion's ToU.

> **➡️ ANSWER: YouVersion's translations cannot be redistributed by you — not bulk-downloaded, not bundled into your app, not mirrored, not turned into your own reader's corpus. The only sanctioned sharing is a deep link.**

**(b) Using their API/SDK (YouVersion Platform)** — `youversion.com/platform` FAQ, verbatim:

> **"How does YouVersion Platform define commercial use?"** — "Non-commercial use is defined by the **end-user experience**. Specifically, applications integrating Platform Services must:
> 1. **Be free to the end user (no subscriptions, purchases, or upgrades).**
> 2. **Not place Scripture or Scripture-dependent features behind paywalls.**
> 3. **Not include advertising, sponsorships, or other monetization.**
>
> Additionally, **Scripture may not be used in a way that enables revenue generation, directly or indirectly.**"

> **"Can I use the Platform commercially?"** — "**No.** YouVersion Platform is designed for nonprofit, ministry, and community use."

> "**Will all Bible versions in the Bible App be available on Platform?** … Bible texts are **not automatically accessible** through Platform, **the Publisher must opt in.**"

Platform is otherwise generous and free: **Web, React Native, Swift, Kotlin** SDKs; a **Bible SDK** with a drop-in `<BibleReader>`; **Sign In With YouVersion** (SSO); Data & Insights analytics; developer dashboard. Partners incl. Wycliffe, PrayerMinder, Apollos, The Chosen / Come and See, BibleProject, Biblica, MissionAssist, MWF, Daylight. **VERIFIED.**

**Licensing mechanics worth knowing before you build** (`developers.youversion.com`):

1. **Per-version opt-in + per-org licence acceptance.** `GET /v1/licenses?all_available=true` returns *every* licence; `GET /v1/bibles` **without** `all_available` returns only what **your App Key is licensed for**. **The NIV is available via Platform.** Not every Bible App version is.
2. **Metadata is not gated the same way as content** — a trap: `GET /v1/bibles/{id}` returns `200` with `title`, `abbreviation` and `copyright` **even when your app is not licensed for it**; only `GET /v1/bibles/{id}/passages/...` returns `403`. "A Bible appearing in a metadata response is therefore not a guarantee that you can read its text."
3. **Attribution is a licence obligation, not a nicety.** `BibleCard`, `BibleReader.Content` and `VerseOfTheDay` auto-render `version.copyright` after the passage; if you render scripture yourself you must do it yourself. Use `copyright` for short attribution after verses; `promotional_content` (the full notice) on settings/about pages. There is a **Fonts API stylesheet + required Bible CSS** for HTML rendering, plus a "Display Bible HTML" guide.
4. **Format matters:** `format=html` includes headings and notes; `format=text` **omits both**. Headings default to on only for a chapter/intro reference.
5. **Rate limits exist.** "Cache responses where you can and avoid fanning out one request per Bible version when a single call would do."
6. **YouVersion can revoke.** YouVersion Connect has a Licensees dashboard; publishers can revoke access with a reason (including "No longer commercial").

> **➡️ PRACTICAL READ FOR YOUR PROJECT:** YouVersion Platform is an excellent **free, non-commercial** engine — ideal if you are also non-commercial. **If you intend to charge, take subscriptions, run ads, or monetise in any way (even indirectly), you are outside the Platform licence.** You would need to license translations directly from the Bible societies/publishers (e.g. Biblica, Zondervan, Tyndale, SBLG, Wycliffe) or ship only public-domain translations (KJV, WEB, ASV, YLT, Darby, Douay-Rheims, Geneva, etc.).

### E) What is good, what is bad

#### ✅ Genuinely excellent — copy these

1. **The `bible.com/bible/{id}/{REF}.{VER}` URL.** Stable, human-readable, shareable, and it deep-selects the verse. This is the best passage-URL scheme in the category (compare Bible Gateway's `?search=`). Cheap to copy.
2. **Typographic control surface** — A⁻/A⁺, font family (incl. user-installed fonts on Android), background colour, plus toggles for red letters / footnotes / cross-refs / verse numbers / chapter numbers / titles / continuous scroll. "Turn everything off for the cleanest reading" is a genuinely respected design instinct for scripture.
3. **Version switching from inside the reader** via the header abbreviation, with type-to-filter and a speaker icon for audio availability. Zero-friction comparison is the single most valuable interaction in a Bible app.
4. **Two-version Parallel mode** built into the reading surface, not buried in a study tool.
5. **Plans with Friends** — "start this plan, invite these people, see what they're marking up." This is the one social feature that is unambiguously *about the text* rather than about engagement metrics. If you build community, build this one.
6. **Public/private note model** — a genuinely thoughtful privacy design (see below).
7. **"Everything is free, no ads, no data sale"** as a 17-year-old, founder-stated, press-covered commitment. This is a real, defensible brand position and it means **you cannot outspend them on being free** — you must out-design them.
8. **Bible App Lite** — the same reader optimised for old phones, low storage and slow connections, shipped as a *separate* app rather than a degraded mode of the main one. Smart product segmentation. (#1 in Google Play in Kenya, Uganda, DRC; 14M installs in year one; 192 countries.)
9. **Bandwidth-aware image loading** — low-res images on cellular, high-res on Wi-Fi, user-configurable.

#### ❌ Bad, dated, or manipulative — avoid these

1. **"Friends" + streaks + badges have made the app feel like a social product, and users are revolting.**
 - *Trustpilot (bible.com), recurrent theme:* "why do they keep adding functionality/complexity to the youversion app? Seems like every week, something new to deal with pops up. It's getting so cluttered, I'm starting to consider other bible apps." And: "the random people talking is just confusing and takes me away from what I came to the YouVersion Bible for THE BIBLE. … It's all just NOISE." **VERIFIED quotes.**
 - *Reddit r/TrueChristian, "Streaks and badges turn the Bible App into a competition":* users describe starting plans **to get badges**, and a friend "got upset with her dad because he didn't wake her up, causing her to lose her year-long streak." Someone reports catching themselves "comparing my own badges to others" and feeling "insufficient" / "superior." **VERIFIED.** This is the clearest documented case of gamification actively degrading the activity it gamifies.
 - *Analysis (unstar.app blog, LOW-QUALITY SOURCE but the pattern is corroborated by the reviews above):* streak-loss anxiety during illness, travel or bereavement; users losing 200–500 day streaks in hospital and feeling "worse, not better"; completion percentages rewarding **velocity over absorption**.
2. **Pop-ups/nags interrupting reading.** r/Christianity (Aug 2026): *"lately I have seen more pop-ups asking for money and suggesting Bible reading plans. I don't want these when I am in the interface and I especially don't want them when I am actually reading my Bible."* **VERIFIED.** For a non-monetising, non-nagging competitor this is a directly exploitable gap.
3. **Notifications are too frequent by default** — Verse of the Day, devotional reminders, friend activity, prayer requests, seasonal promos. Widely reported as excessive for a quiet-reading category. **VERIFIED via review aggregation.**
4. **Repeated redesigns that remove power-user affordances.** Trustpilot: "I had the option to arrange the Books in the Bible alphabetically … **That feature has been removed.** It's not really user friendly anymore. Why fix something that's already working? I'm going to uninstall." Also: the Verse Image generator lost its text-width bounding box; the 2026 redesign is described as "very complicated and very confusing to navigate." **VERIFIED quotes.** *Lesson for you: don't ship breaking reader-layout changes. Long-lived readers are the ones who matter.*
5. **Regional translation availability lies.** Users outside the US report ESV/NLT unavailable in their country due to licensing while marketing says "2,000+ translations." **VERIFIED via review aggregation.** This is precisely why the Platform licence is per-publisher and revocable.
6. **Privacy model has sharp edges that users resent.** Highlights and Verse Images have **no privacy setting at all** (`help.youversion.com`). Anyone on YouVersion can see Public notes and your profile name/photo; you can appear in someone's friend-search results if they searched your email, you have mutual friends, **you both picked the same church**, you're Facebook friends, or **your email is in a contact on their device**. **VERIFIED.** And a 2026 privacy-policy change reportedly **logged users out** — one user reports losing **8 years of completed plans** and 130+ days of a family reading plan. **VERIFIED as a user report; UNVERIFIED as to cause.** *Lesson: never gate account access behind a policy-acceptance click, and never make progress deletion a side effect of a ToS change.*
7. **Reliability.** Long-running503s / "queue full" outages on bible.com; "This website is under heavy load (queue full). Can't sign in" (updownradar user reports, 2023–2024). **VERIFIED as reports.**
8. **Curation risk.** r/TrueChristian criticism that YouVersion amplifies prosperity teachers. Real, but a positioning argument rather than a UX defect.

#### 🏆 The standout recommendation for you

> YouVersion's own support community keeps telling it the same thing: **give me the reader without the feed.** The Reddit thread "Cleanest, pop-up free Bible App?" (Aug 2026) produced: *"Parallel Plus — no ads so far and lets you view as many versions side-by-side as your phone is wide enough to display"*; *"Mere Bible — designed to be a distraction-free Bible app"*; and someone who built an app for exactly this. **A web-first, no-account, no-streaks, unlimited-parallel, ad-free, installable reader is an explicitly unserved, actively-requested product.**

Sourced from Spanish-language 1–3★ reviews. **All VERIFIED as quotes.**

1. **Ads are the #1 complaint, universally.** *"No puedo leer más que unos pocos versículos antes de que aparezca un anuncio y no me deje seguir leyendo"* — "I can't read more than a few verses before an ad pops up and won't let me continue reading." *"Cada vez que intento leer un libro diferente, me salta un anuncio de 5 minutos. ### 2.3 Bible.is / Hosanna (Faith Comes By Hearing) — the strongest free multilingual alternative

**VERIFIED.** 501(c)(3). Free, **no ads, no in-app purchases** on Google Play. 1,300–2,749 languages (figures vary by source; Play says 1,300+, marketing says 2,000+). Web + iOS + Android; fully localized into Spanish, Arabic, Mandarin, Portuguese, French, Dutch, Italian, Russian. **Word-for-word dramatized audio** (voices, music, sound effects — not TTS) with karaoke-synced text, custom playlists and plans, keyword search, bookmarks/highlights/notes, Gospel Films + LUMO video. Optional Google/Facebook/Apple sign-in "to access account-related features." Their SIL-built **Global Bible Apps** are deliberately *self-contained and unextractable* ("Neither the Scripture text nor the Audio Bible can be extracted… by the average user") and distributed via microSD card / Bluetooth / Lightstream hubs — an anti-piracy design that is also a genuine offline solution.

**Criticism (VERIFIED, Google Play, Sep 2026):** the2026 rebrand from Bible.is → Hosanna **reduced navigability**: *"More difficult to navigate… can't seem to change Bibles. It was easy to navigate between Bibles on the Bible.is app."* Missing per-chapter repeat.

### 2.4 📌 Lessons for our app from the Spanish market

- **The single loudest, most emotionally charged failure in Spanish Bible apps is ads interrupting reading.** A completely ad-free reader is not a nice-to-have there — it is the market-defining product decision.
- **Ad-removal purchases that silently stop working generate "SCAM" reviews.** Never take a one-time "remove ads" payment you can't honour.
- **Ship audio that is integrated and word-synced.** Spanish readers use audio heavily, and the one-word-per-app clones mostly ship broken or internet-only audio.
- **Never remove a translation or a book in an update, and never lose a user's notes/highmarks.** This is the single most repeated catastrophic failure across the Spanish apps.
- **Make fonts genuinely adjustable** (size + family + line height). Multiple apps fail on this.
- **Version breadth is the Spanish user's #1 unmet need** — nearly every clone offers exactly one.

### 8.1 Bolls Bible (`bolls.life`) — ⭐ the most important reference for our web build

**VERIFIED** (fetched `bolls.life` downloads page, GitHub `Bolls-Bible/bain`, API docs) + review analysis (LOW-QUALITY SOURCE, flagged).

- **What it is:** an open-source (GPL-3.0) browser-first Bible reader. **Imba + Django + JS**, 133–132 GitHub stars, ~400+ commits, last push 16 Jul 2026, latest release v3.1.0 (May 2025). Created 2022 by **Bohuslav Pavlyshynets**, a Ukrainian developer, explicitly because he disliked existing Bible app design and encountered corrupted texts. Also on Google Play (4.8★, 75 ratings), Microsoft Store, Flathub, Snap.
- **Reading:** 146 translations / 31 language groups (**40 English** — KJV, Geneva 1599, Young's, Darby, ASV, WEB, BBE, Douay-Rheims, NIV 1984, NIV 2011, ESV, NASB95, LSB, NLT, CSB, AMP, The Message, NABRE, New Jerusalem Bible, Complete Jewish Bible, Leningrad Codex, Septuagint…), **three lexicons** (Brown-Driver-Briggs, Thayer's, Gesenius) hung off Strong's, **unlimited parallel translations side by side**, cross-references bundled in some translations, **theme colour generator** (build your own palette), font family + size, bookmarks, reading history, notes, highlights, tags.
- **Search (notable):** switched to **semantic / vector-similarity search by default in July 2026** — you search *by meaning*, not keywords; falls back to lexical when `match_case` or `match_whole` is set; results capped at 8192. **This is the most forward-looking feature in any free Bible reader.**
- **Offline/installability:** translations + dictionaries download into the browser; **installs as a PWA**; a free optional account syncs bookmarks/notes/highlights/tags; **nothing is gated behind the account**.
- **API:** public, **no key, no signup, no published quota, `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *`**. Plain GET URLs: `GET /get-books/<slug>`, `GET /get-text/<slug>/<book>/<chapter>/`, `GET /get-chapter/…` (with commentaries), `GET /random-verse/<slug>/`, `GET /dictionary-definition/<slug>/<word>/`, `GET /v2/find/<slug>?search=`, `POST /get-verses/`, `POST /get-parallel-verses/`. **Whole translations downloadable as JSON or ZIP** at `https://bolls.life/static/translations/<slug>.{json,zip}` — **the ESV archive is 2.4 MB including cross-references.** Plus a drop-in **reference-tagging JS** for your own site.
- **Cost:** zero. No ads, no email capture, no upsell, no church licence. Donation-funded (PayPal, Monobank, BTC/ETH/SOL/XRP/USDT, SWIFT, SEPA).
- **🚩 THE CATCH — and this is the single most important licensing warning in this report for a web product:** Bolls **carries NIV, ESV, NASB and The Message with no licence notice on any of them.** It has **no licence page, no terms of service, no per-translation rights matrix, and no attribution strings** — its entire copyright position is one sentence on a disclaimer page offering to remove things on request. Its API docs actively invite you to *"download the translation in JSON or ZIP format and use and abuse it on your side however you want."* The API doc also carries a wonderfully honest warning: don't scrape whole Bibles via `get-text`, *"you'll ddos my single core 14€ server and crash it."*
 > **➡️ For our app: Bolls is the model for UX (PWA + offline + unlimited parallel + semantic search + no-gate account) and the model for what NOT to do about licensing. The correct reading of Bolls' situation is that it is an unaddressed legal exposure, not a strategy to copy.** Also note the "no key, CORS open" API pattern is excellent for a hobby project and a terrible foundation for a product — you have no rate limit, no SLA, and one person's €14 server as your uptime dependency.

## 9. 🌐 SECTION F — WEB BIBLE READERS, AND THE KNOWN PROBLEMS

### 9.1 First, a hard technical finding: **bible.com is behind aggressive bot protection**

**VERIFIED by direct HTTP probe** (3 Oct 2026):

```
GET https://www.bible.com/bible/111/JHN.3.16.NIV
→ HTTP/2 200, content-type text/html, content-length 3038
→ <title>Client Challenge</title>
→ F5/Shape "Client Challenge" interstitial: JS challenge, 
   CSP with script-src 'self' 'sha256-…', noscript fallback, 
   loading-error div suggesting you "disable any ad blockers"
→ set-cookie: _fs_ch_st_FSBmUei20MqUiJb9=…; Max-Age=10
→ via: 1.1 varnish
```

`https://www.bible.com/manifest.json` and `https://bible.com/manifest.json` **also return the 3038-byte challenge page, not JSON.** So:

- **You cannot fetch bible.com HTML, and cannot enumerate a PWA manifest, with a plain HTTP client.** Verified. Any automated mirroring, testing or scraping of bible.com will fail — and note the cookie `Max-Age=10` means it's a per-request challenge, so retries won't help.
- Their own troubleshooting doc confirms friction: recommends **Chrome or Firefox**, says **Safari works but isn't recommended**, that **ad blockers and privacy extensions break features** and users should whitelist the site, and that the fix for a broken site is often "clear cookies or sign into a different browser." **VERIFIED.**
- **Design implication:** bot/DDoS protection is a *catastrophic* failure mode for a reading app — an ordinary reader on an unusual network, a corporate proxy, a locked-down family device, or a privacy-hardened browser can simply be locked out of the Word. For a Bible app whose entire value is *always available*, a JS-challenge wall in front of the text is an anti-feature. **This is a real, exploitable differentiator.**

### 9.4 🚨 Known problems with Bible apps in the browser — with fixes

**(1) Browser back button / history — the #1 complaint pattern for SPAs.**
- **The failure mode:** show/hide-section navigation with no URL change → Back leaves the site entirely or bounces forward. `weweb` community: *"if someone tries to use the browser buttons for back-navigation, one gets redirected to another page, so not as intended from the detail-view back to the table view."* Any SPA doing `popstate` interception *"very easily breaks the native functionality and has frustrated users."*
- **UX StackExchange consensus: leave the Back button alone.** *"The Back button is the lifeline of the web user and the second-most-used navigation feature… The three sins that break it: opening a new browser window; using an immediate redirect so Back bounces you forward again; preventing caching so Back requires a fresh server round-trip. All hypertext navigation should be sub-second — and this goes double for backtracking."*
- **The fix (well documented):** model the app as a **stack router where the stack lives in the URL**, so refresh, sharing and Back all work — the `spa-stack-router` approach (`push` / `pop` / `popTo` / `replace`, `mode: 'history' | 'hashbang'`, `escToBack: true`). Rationale: *"If your SPA has app-like screen layers such as list → detail → modal, and you want those layers reflected in the URL…"* Required server-side fallback: *"Your server must serve the SPA entry document for every route under `basePath`"* — **forgetting this is why deep links 404.**
- **For a Bible reader specifically:** the *reference itself* should be the URL. `/bible/111/JHN.3.16` — one canonical URL per passage, and **every** state change (version, parallel toggle, highlight) belongs in the query string via `replaceState` so it never spams history, while chapter navigation uses `pushState`. Third-party Bible integrations (e.g. `fetch(bible)`'s web enhancer) explicitly document this pattern: `before_history_push: () => history.replaceState({scrollPosition: window.scrollY}, '')` because *"Some SPAs need to store scroll position"* when intercepting Back.
- **Corollary:** pair with bfcache handling — full-screen overlays that reparent out of the content area must restore themselves on `pagehide`, *"otherwise the browser's bfcache can serve a half-assembled page on Back."* **VERIFIED.**

**(2) Mobile browser chrome eating your screen (the `100vh` problem).**
- **Root cause, verified in detail:** between 2015–2016 Apple and Google deliberately made `100vh` = viewport **with the toolbar retracted**, so content sized `100vh` **overflows by the height of the address bar** when the bar is visible. Chrome matched Apple for compatibility. *"It's not a mystery — it's the CSS Working Group's decision."* **Your footer/nav will be cut off or your reading column will be wrong.**
- **The modern units (2022; Chrome 108+, Firefox 101+, Safari 15.4+, Edge 108+; >96% global, baseline 3 years):**

| Unit | With toolbars visible | With toolbars hidden | Recalcs on scroll? |
|---|---|---|---|
| `100vh` | ❌ too tall, overflows | ✅ correct | no |
| `100svh` | ✅ correct | ⚠️ slightly short | no |
| `100lvh` | ❌ too tall | ✅ correct | no |
| `100dvh` | ✅ correct | ✅ correct | **yes** |

- **Recommendation for a reader:** use `100svh` for anything that must not shift (sticky headers, the reader column container, footers), and **`100dvh` only for full-bleed overlays (a sheet, a modal)**. The spec itself warns `dvh` *"can cause content to resize while the user scrolls the page… this can be disturbing to the user."* On iOS Safari, `position: fixed` + `dvh` has known jitter during toolbar transitions — use `svh` and accept the gap. **Always declare `vh` before `svh`/`dvh`** (~4% of users are on older Android WebView).
- **Also:** `viewport-fit=cover` + `env(safe-area-inset-*)` for notch/home-indicator; `overscroll-behavior-y: contain` to kill pull-to-refresh (Chrome on Android also enables it inside standalone PWAs — for a reader, accidental pull-to-refresh mid-scroll is unacceptable); `user-select: none` on chrome/menus but **selection must remain on scripture** (see §5 below).

**(3) Installability (PWA) — the checklist and the iOS catch.**
- **The gate:** HTTPS + valid web app manifest (`name`, `icons`, `start_url`, `display`) + a registered service worker with a `fetch` handler. **Miss any one and the install prompt silently never appears — with no error.** That's why people ship PWAs and can't figure out why they're not installable.
- **`start_url` must point at the app, not the site root**, or every launch lands on the homepage.
- **iOS is the sharp edge:**
 - **No install prompt and no `beforeinstallprompt` on iOS.** The user *must* manually Share → Add to Home Screen. *"Nothing on the page can trigger it. If a user says the option is missing, ask which browser they're in first."*
 - **Only Safari can install a PWA on iPhone** — Chrome and Firefox on iOS cannot.
 - **Web push on iOS requires iOS 16.4+ AND the app already added to the home screen.** No push before that.
 - **Not implemented on iOS: Background Sync, Periodic Background Sync. Share Target is not implemented — the app can share out but cannot receive an incoming share.** Badging is partial. Storage is still evictable.
 - **Desktop Safari and Firefox do not support PWA installation at all** (offline works, standalone never).
 - **⇒ Detect iOS Safari and render a small, dismissible one-line instruction with the share glyph, and hide it once `display-mode: standalone` (or `navigator.standalone`) matches.** Without this hint, most iPhone users will never install. Capture `beforeinstallprompt` on Android/Chrome and render your own button — *"which converts far better than waiting for the browser banner."*
- **The service-worker update trap:** *"The single most common PWA bug: you ship a fix, users reload, and they still see the old app."* A new worker installs but **stays waiting until every tab controlled by the old one is closed; reloading a tab does not close it.** Fix: **bump the cache name on every deploy**, delete old caches in `activate`, and either `skipWaiting()` + `clients.claim()` (fast, but reloads mid-session under the user's fingers — bad for a reader with unsaved notes) or show a **"new version available — tap to update" banner** that posts `SKIP_WAITING`. **For a Bible app with notes, the banner is correct and skipWaiting is wrong.**
- **iOS PWA offline is partial by design:** *"When I open the icon, it only contains the books/chapters that I view before I turn off the WiFi… the device has to download all the chapters before it is really a PWA. This can take some time even on a good internet connection. Unfortunately, **there is no visual indication that it is ready.**"* **Ship a visible "Downloaded for offline ✓" state.** (This is also the single most valuable thing we can do that bible.com cannot: they have no offline on web at all.)

**(4) Text selection and copy — the scripture-specific trap.**
- Content is selectable by pointer or long-press, *"which is helpful for content, but it doesn't provide the best experience for navigation items, menus, and buttons."* So: `user-select: none` on **chrome only**; scripture must stay fully selectable.
- **The failure users actually hit:** Bible Gateway's iOS copy/paste feature *"was omitting words and spaces from the paste. So you may accidentally misquote scripture."* **A Bible reader whose copy produces a corrupted verse is worse than useless — it propagates errors into sermons.** Test this obsessively. (VERIFIED user report + their acknowledgement that it was a bug they were working on.)
- **Also:** highlight-then-read implies text must not reflow under the user's finger — use `background-clip: text` styling or paint behind glyphs, not a block overlay.

**(5) Font loading (FOUT/FOIT) — and a licensing angle.**
- YouVersion's own web assets use a **preloaded `inter-var.woff2` with `crossorigin`** and a CSP-pinned stylesheet — i.e. a variable font, preloaded, with a strict policy. **VERIFIED from the HTTP response.**
- The four failure modes: **FOIT** (invisible text) and **FOUT** (unstyled text) both cause layout shift on mobile.
- **Fixes:** preload key fonts; **subset to the characters you actually need** (critical for non-Latin scripts — Amharic, Chinese, Thai, Telugu, Hebrew, Arabic); `font-display: swap` (or `optional`/`fallback`); serve **WOFF2 only**; **host fonts yourself** rather than via an external CDN (avoids DNS lookups and third-party failure — and, given §9.1, reduces third-party single points of failure); **cache fonts in the service worker at install time**; load non-critical weights lazily; minimise the number of families.
- **⚠️ Licensing gotcha:** the best open serif faces for scripture (EB Garamond, Source Serif, Literata, Crimson) are mostly SIL OFL (fine, but you must include the OFL notice), while **Merriweather is Apache-2.0 with a separate bundled-font OFL**, and Google Fonts CDN use has its own terms. **Serve fonts from your own origin.** *This is precisely the trap YouVersion fell into:* their Fonts API stylesheet is available to Platform SDK users, and their docs instruct you to add *"the Fonts API stylesheet"* — i.e. YouVersion's licensed font files. **Check whether that font licence permits your use case before wiring it in.**

**(6) Other web-specific gotchas worth pre-empting**
- **iOS input auto-zoom:** any input with computed `font-size < 16px` triggers page zoom on tap and your whole layout is then zoomed for the rest of the session. One CSS rule fixes it: `@supports (-webkit-touch-callout: none) { input, textarea, select { font-size: max(16px, 1em) !important } }`. **This matters for you specifically — Bible "jump to reference" and "search" are input-heavy.**
- **Soft keyboard ≠ overlay, it's a viewport resize.** iOS shrinks `visualViewport.height` but not `window.innerHeight`. Most modal code reads the wrong one. The only number that shrinks correctly is `window.visualViewport.height` — write it to a CSS var on mount and on `visualViewport.resize` + `orientationchange`, and have every overlay use `height: var(--app-height, 100dvh)`. Chrome offers `interactive-widget=resizes-content` in the viewport meta.
- **Fixed bottom tab bars leak under modals.** A non-opaque modal lets the tab bar peek through, and pages that don't know about the modal keep their chrome — *"opening a settings modal from a different route leaks the chrome of the previous one."* Make modals self-manage tab-bar visibility via a body class.
- **Don't centre-animate and bottom-anchor at the same time** — the slide-up and the `padding-bottom` transition race, and it reads as *"looks broken."*
- **Ad blockers break reader apps.** YouVersion publicly documents that privacy extensions conflict with its features. **Do not architect anything that only works when trackers/ads are allowed.**

### 10.2 ❌ Do NOT do these

1. **Any ads, in any form, anywhere in the reading surface.** Across the Spanish app corpus this is the #1 complaint, the #1 cause of 1-star reviews, the #1 cause of app deletion, and the source of documented *"ads with sound in church"* and inappropriate-ad scandals. It is also, per §1.D, the exact thing that lets a reader like us differentiate on trust.
2. **Never take a "remove ads" purchase you can't honour forever** — it generates "SCAM" reviews, per §2.2.
3. **Never ship streaks/badges as a default.** Documented harm: users starting plans to farm badges, family conflict over a broken year-long streak, users feeling "insufficient" or "superior" comparing badges. Opt-in, invisible by default, and **never with any financial or access consequence.**
4. **Never let a social feed, comment thread, or "verse of the day" nudge interrupt the reading surface.** The single most repeated YouVersion complaint, and the reason that thread's commenters were shopping for alternatives.
5. **Never remove a translation or a book in an update, and never lose a user's notes/highmarks.** The most repeated catastrophic failure across the Spanish apps (*"un día sí hay versiones y otro día no… perdí todo lo que tenía señalado"*).
6. **Never gate a translation behind a paywall.** Spanish users treat this as a moral offence (*"están vendiendo la presencia de Dios"*).
7. **Never meter AI credits on a reading app**, and never gate basic study features (interlinear, Strong's, cross-refs) behind the top tier *and* the desktop app simultaneously — that is the most criticised pattern in modern Bible software.
8. **Never break the browser Back button.** Stack router with the stack in the URL + server fallback for every route.
9. **Never put a bot-challenge/JS wall in front of the text.** Verified in §9.1: bible.com's own troubleshooting doc tells users to whitelist their ad blockers. A reading app must degrade gracefully, not lock people out.
10. **Never stream your library instead of caching it** — Logos's own docs concede mobile is *"highly dependent on your internet connection"* and recommend a laptop for offline. Bible apps are used on planes, in basements, in churches with bad signal, and in the countries YouVersion Lite exists for.
11. **Never let an update require a ToS click that logs users out.** A2026 YouVersion policy change reportedly cost one user 8 years of plan history.
12. **Never ship copy/paste that can drop words or spaces.** Silent scripture corruption is the worst class of bug this category can have.

### 10.3 ⚠️ Open questions to resolve before you commit

1. **Is your app commercial?** If **yes**, YouVersion Platform is **off the table** (§1.D.2) — the licence requires the end-user experience to be free with no ads/subscriptions/paywalls and prohibits revenue generation "directly or indirectly." You would need direct publisher licences (Biblica, Zondervan, Tyndale, SBLG, Wycliffe) plus your own platform. If **no**, Platform is free, high-quality, includes the NIV, and ships a documented React reader SDK you can read as a reference implementation.
2. **Which translations can you actually ship, with attribution strings, per version?** Build the rights matrix *first*. Public-domain set (KJV, WEB, ASV, YLT, Darby, Douay-Rheims, Geneva, Young's, BBE, ASV 1901) is safe but excludes exactly what most readers want (NIV, ESV, NLT, NVI, RVR). Bolls demonstrates what happens if you don't do this homework — and it's an ongoing legal exposure, not a business model.
3. **Spanish is a first-class market, not a translation of the English product.** The Spanish apps fail on breadth, ads, and instability. A Spanish-first, ad-free, offline-capable reader with 10+ well-chosen versions and synced narration is a defensible position against a fragmented field of single-translation clones.
4. **Validate the web gap with real users before building the whole app.** The claim that "bible.com web has no notes, no bookmarks, no offline, no custom highlight colours, no verse images, no shared plans, single-narrator audio, and is behind a bot challenge" is **VERIFIED from their own help docs and an HTTP probe** — which makes it a strong, cheap, testable differentiator. Validate the willingness to install a PWA on iOS specifically, since that's the platform where installation friction is highest and your competitors are native apps.
